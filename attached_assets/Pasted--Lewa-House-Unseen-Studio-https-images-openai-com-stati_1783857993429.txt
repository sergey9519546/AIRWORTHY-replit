* Dark navy interface

The useful references are coming from **high-end digital studios building spatial systems, technical products, and immersive interfaces**, not SaaS galleries.

# The strongest references

## 1. Air Space Intelligence × Studio Freight

This is the closest strategic reference for AIRWORTHY—not because of the aviation name, but because Studio Freight built a proprietary visual language from the product’s actual operating environment.

Instead of generic dark technology styling, they moved toward a high-visibility light system informed by air-traffic control and cockpit instrumentation. Their palette, monospaced typography, predictive timeline, topographic data, fictional geography, and modular command-center imagery all reinforce the idea of **foresight**. ([Studio Freight][1])

### What AIRWORTHY should learn

* Build the identity from **property intelligence**, not luxury real estate.
* Use colors that come from zoning maps, weather radar, parcel records, topography, and forecasting.
* Make the interface communicate **what is likely to happen**, not merely display current metrics.
* Use invented but plausible spatial environments for marketing imagery.
* Turn prediction into a visual behavior.

### Do not copy

* Military-command-center aesthetics
* Cockpit graphics
* Their exact mono typography
* Their color system
* Their timeline treatment

The principle to borrow is: **the product’s function must generate the brand language.**

## 2. MTA Live Subway Map × Work & Co

Work & Co did not place a dashboard beside a conventional map. They rebuilt the map as a live digital system that redraws itself from real-time information. Service changes are expressed visually instead of being explained through paragraphs and surrounding UI. ([Work & Co][2])

### What AIRWORTHY should learn

The map should not be a background covered with cards. It should **be the interface**.

For example:

* Revenue potential changes the terrain.
* Seasonality alters the map over time.
* Regulation risk visibly interrupts otherwise attractive markets.
* Property supply appears as density.
* Selecting a property reshapes the surrounding comparable network.
* A timeline lets the user inspect projected performance month by month.

This is substantially more sophisticated than placing `$92K` bubbles over Mapbox.

---

## 3. Lewa House × Unseen Studio

Unseen made the map the center of a nonlinear discovery experience. Information can be opened without leaving the spatial environment, while purposeful animation, rich media, and gamified interactions create exploration rather than conventional navigation. The terrain itself was custom-built in WebGL from topographic information and high-resolution tiled textures. ([Unseen Studio®][3])

### What AIRWORTHY should learn

The property-search experience can feel like **surveying a living market** rather than operating a filter panel.

Possible AIRWORTHY behavior:

* Smooth movement between national, market, neighborhood, parcel, and property levels
* Buildings subtly extruded according to projected income
* Terrain responding to the selected analytical layer
* Property photographs embedded into geographic space
* Neighborhood insights discovered spatially
* Progressive information instead of permanent sidebars

This is the correct reference for making AIRWORTHY feel exploratory without becoming decorative.

---

## 4. Lightship × Locomotive

Locomotive redesigned Lightship as a product-led journey using progressive disclosure, navigable layers, immersive 3D, and interactive explanations of systems and performance. The experience moves between inspiration and technical understanding while retaining a scalable design system. ([Locomotive][4])

### What AIRWORTHY should learn

The landing page should **demonstrate the analysis progressively**.

Instead of:

> Hero → logos → feature cards → testimonial → CTA

Use:

1. Enter a property.
2. Reveal the building.
3. Establish its location and market context.
4. Build the comparable set around it.
5. Animate the expected revenue envelope.
6. Reveal the major performance drivers.
7. Deliver the AIRWORTHY verdict.

The marketing site should behave like a controlled product demonstration.

---

## 5. Daylight × Basement Studio

Basement approached Daylight as an emotional, high-performance product experience rather than a collection of sections. The environment changes through light and shadow to communicate how the product fits into a user’s life, while the site remains tied to commercial and UX objectives. ([Basement Studio][5])

### What AIRWORTHY should learn

AIRWORTHY needs an atmospheric system that is **generated by revenue behavior**.

A property could transition through:

* Low-season morning
* High-demand weekend
* Summer peak
* Winter compression
* Event-driven surge
* Downside scenario

Architecture, occupancy, lighting, map activity, and forecast data could change together. This would make the estimate feel temporal and tangible.

## 6. Felt

Felt is a better functional benchmark than conventional real-estate platforms because it treats geospatial work as a direct-manipulation environment. The map remains central while uploading, analyzing, visualizing, collaborating, and building dashboards occur around it. ([Felt][6])

### What AIRWORTHY should learn

* Direct manipulation over filter forms
* Contextual toolbars
* Layers rather than dashboard pages
* Visible analytical provenance
* Spatial workflows that remain understandable
* Advanced capabilities disclosed only when needed

AIRWORTHY should feel closer to **Felt simplified for rental investment decisions** than to an Airbnb calculator dressed as a Bloomberg terminal.

---

## 7. Legend × Studio Freight

Studio Freight developed Legend through the intersection of NASA graphics, Swiss wayfinding, surreal landscapes, geographic coordinates, and product UI embedded inside environmental scenes. The result avoids cold crypto conventions while retaining technical precision. ([Studio Freight][7])

### What AIRWORTHY should learn

Product UI does not always need to float inside laptop mockups.

AIRWORTHY data can be integrated into:

* Aerial landscapes
* Architectural cutaways
* Parcel diagrams
* Seasonal environments
* Property photographs
* Physical report-like compositions

The interface and the property should appear to belong to one visual world.

# The correct AIRWORTHY direction

## **Predictive Property Atlas**

Not luxury SaaS.
Not aviation-themed software.
Not a conventional real-estate dashboard.

AIRWORTHY should feel like a **living spatial model of a property’s earning future**.

### Core visual concept

Every property creates a unique **forecast terrain**.

Revenue, occupancy, seasonality, confidence, regulation, competition, and operating risk are translated into a layered spatial field around the building.

The user is not merely reading an estimate. They are inspecting the forces that produced it.

---

# New visual language

## Canvas

Use a high-visibility, daylight interface:

* Warm survey-paper background
* Near-black typography
* Desaturated satellite and cadastral textures
* High-visibility forecasting colors
* Occasional deep-night analytical scenes
* No gold
* No purple gradient
* No generic “premium dark mode”

## Typography

Use three coordinated voices:

* **Editorial grotesk** for product narrative
* **Technical mono** for coordinates, assumptions, and source data
* **Condensed display face** for revenue and status

Do not use a luxury fashion serif for every headline.

## Graphic materials

Build the system around:

* Topographic contours
* Parcel outlines
* Cadastral grids
* Seasonal solar paths
* Occupancy traces
* Comparable-property vectors
* Forecast frames
* Confidence boundaries
* Geographic coordinates
* Architectural sections
* Revenue elevation fields

These are native to the product. They are not decorative motifs.

# Cover page

## Opening state

Nearly empty interface:

```text
AIRWORTHY                                     Sign in

What could this property earn?

[ Enter an address, listing URL, or parcel ID               → ]
```

Behind the address field is a restrained, continuously moving geographic field—not a mansion photograph.

Once an address is entered, the page transforms.

## Analysis sequence

1. The map locates the parcel.
2. The building rises from the terrain.
3. Nearby comparable properties emerge.
4. Their connections form a visible evidence network.
5. Seasonal waves move through the scene.
6. A forecast range resolves.
7. The verdict appears.

```text
2209 Ridgeway Drive
Austin, Texas

$78,400 — $94,100
EXPECTED ANNUAL REVENUE

AIRWORTHY / 82
Strong potential · moderate seasonality exposure
```

The user has experienced the product before creating an account.

# Map-search page

## Full-screen spatial workspace

The map occupies almost the complete viewport.

No permanent oversized left filter column.

### Top rail

```text
Austin, Texas          Market     Property     Compare

Revenue potential ▾    4 guests ▾    Entire home ▾
```

### Bottom analytical timeline

```text
JAN ─ FEB ─ MAR ─ APR ─ MAY ─ JUN ─ JUL ─ AUG ─ SEP ─ OCT ─ NOV ─ DEC
```

Dragging across months transforms the map:

* Building elevation changes with expected revenue
* Demand density moves
* Event zones appear
* ADR pressure changes
* Selected-property projections update
* Confidence bands expand or contract

### Selection behavior

Selecting a property should not open a generic popup.

The surrounding map dims slightly, nearby comparable properties become connected, and an inspection strip emerges:

```text
2209 Ridgeway Drive

$86.2K expected
$78.4K—$94.1K modeled range
31 accepted comparables
High forecast confidence

[Inspect model →]
```

# Property page

## Digital property dossier

The property page should resemble a continuously navigable evidence document rather than a dashboard grid.

### Opening composition

* Large architectural elevation or aerial parcel image
* Property address
* Revenue range
* AIRWORTHY score
* Confidence statement
* Main decision sentence

```text
AIRWORTHY / 82

Strong earning potential, but performance relies on
summer pool demand and weekend-rate optimization.
```

### Forecast terrain

The signature visualization should combine:

* Central revenue estimate
* Probability distribution
* Monthly seasonality
* Downside threshold
* Market benchmark
* User-adjusted scenario

Not a standard bar chart.

### Comparable constellation

The subject property sits at the center.

Each comparable is positioned according to similarity—not merely geographic distance.

Connections indicate:

* Bedroom match
* Amenity match
* Distance
* Listing quality
* Occupancy behavior
* Seasonal similarity
* Pricing similarity

Selecting a comparable reveals why it was accepted or rejected.

### Building cutaway

An interactive architectural section exposes revenue contribution:

```text
POOL                   +$9.8K
FOURTH BEDROOM         +$7.1K
OUTDOOR DINING         +$3.4K
DISTANCE FROM CORE     −$5.2K
SUPPLY GROWTH           −$4.1K
```

That is substantially more distinctive than another “Market insights” card.

# AIRWORTHY’s proprietary interaction

## **Flight Path**

The brand’s aviation meaning should appear once, subtly, through forecasting.

A thin trajectory moves from:

```text
PROPERTY → MARKET CONDITIONS → OPERATING ASSUMPTIONS → REVENUE OUTCOME
```

Changing any assumption bends the trajectory.

* Strong trajectory: **Cleared**
* Sensitive trajectory: **Conditional**
* Unstable trajectory: **Exposed**
* Nonviable trajectory: **Grounded**

This gives AIRWORTHY a coherent metaphor without airplane icons or cockpit cosplay.
